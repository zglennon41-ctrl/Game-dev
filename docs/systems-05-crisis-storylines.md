# Crisis Storylines

Every season carries one crisis, a thirteen-week storyline with three possible endings that the whole server decides by what it does. The ending is written into the world at the season turn and never reversed. Twelve crises make a Charter term, and the twelve below are the first term's.

## The thirteen weeks

| Weeks | Stage | What players see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–3 | Omen | Rumors on Monday boards, a new zone variant, side quests that point at the trouble, the Compact's first notice |
| 4–9 | Pressure | Crisis-tagged quests on every board, weighted by line color; the Ledgers open and publish weekly; guilds declare; houses court |
| 10–12 | Crisis | The climax zones open (Red, with named members at risk); Writ wars legal in Writ season; the Rally final in Rally season |
| 13 | Reckoning | Boards close on the turn day; the Ledgers are read at the lamp hour; the world changes; the Chronicle writes the page |

## How the server decides

Each crisis has three Ledgers, one per ending, published by the Compact every Wednesday so everyone can see where it stands. Weight flows into a Ledger from four sources, and nothing else.

| Source | Weight | Who moves it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crisis-tagged quests completed | Green 1, Amber 3, Red 9 per completion, to the Ledger the quest serves | Any party |
| Guild declarations | The guild's Standing, once, changeable until week 9 | Chartered guilds and above |
| House Favor spent | Favor a player burns at a house for its preferred ending, 1 per point | Anyone with Favor |
| The Convention vote | Third Dark only: one vote per Seat guild and surviving house | The Convention |

At Reckoning the heaviest Ledger wins. A Ledger must hold at least 25% of the total to win outright; if none does, the story's default ending holds, and the Chronicle says the server could not decide, which is its own kind of history.

## What an ending writes

Every ending writes at least one change in each of five places, so no ending is cosmetic.

| Place | Example |
| --- | --- |
| The line map | A line relit, a junction gone dark, a zone added or sealed |
| The houses | A house gains the Charter seat, loses its heir, ends |
| The Archive | An archetype gained, lost, or restricted; an Instructor seat created |
| The roster | A named NPC lives, dies, leaves, or arrives, on every guild's roster that knew them |
| The Chronicle | The season's page, readable by anyone, quoted at the next Omen |

Each crisis is written branch-aware: its Omen quotes the last Reckoning, and three of its quests exist only under one of the previous endings. By crisis 6, servers are already telling different stories.

## Late players and Echoes

A player who arrives in year three did not live crisis 1, but the world they live in is its result. The Chronicle carries each crisis's page, and the Archive offers Echoes: sealed zones that replay a past crisis's climax as it happened on this server, for Marks and lore, never for the Ledger. Echoes are how a new cohort learns why the Cahokia line has a house toll.

## Authoring rules

1. Three endings, all of them playable futures. No ending is the bad ending; each is a world someone wanted.
2. Every ending writes all five places.
3. The default ending is the one that happens when the server does nothing, and it is never the best one.
4. A named NPC at risk is named in the Omen, so the server knows who it is deciding for.
5. The next crisis reads the last one. No reset.
6. Crisis content ships live, season by season; the twelve above are the first term's plan and the later terms learn from the Chronicles of every server.
