# Guilds

A guild is a hearth, a hall, a roster, and a name on the Chronicle. Guilds are founded by classmates at Commencement, rise by holding junctions and winning Rallies, govern themselves under one of three Charters, and are the unit the server's history is written in.

## Founding

Three graduates of one cohort, from three accounts, declare at a Commencement. NPC classmates can stand in for players, so a founding is always possible, and an NPC co-founder is a full member with a life and a vote. The founders choose a name (unique on the server), a mark, and a Charter template. The Academy issues a Writ of Founding, and the guild is a Company: real, chartered to take quests, and homeless.

## Charters

The Charter is how the guild decides. It is chosen at founding and can be changed once a year, at the Thaw, by whatever process the current Charter allows.

| Charter | Who decides | Master chosen by | Suits | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Captaincy | The Master, advised by officers | The founders, then the Master's named heir | Small, fast guilds; one leader | House Carroll likes it; Banneker does not |
| Council | The Master and four officers by majority | The council | Mid-size guilds with a leadership group | Slower Writs; ties go to the Master |
| Commons | Every member, one vote per account | Election each Thaw | Large guilds, cooperatives | The Compact charges a Commons double for junction tolls; the Commons is also the only Charter that can refuse a house Writ without losing Favor |

Each Charter is a real difference in play, not a label: Writs arrive with a decision timer, and under a Commons the vote is the hall's business for a night.

## Halls and junctions

A Company lodges: at a chartered guild's hall as tenants (rent in Notes and a cut of Standing) or at a city hall (free, no hearth of its own, no Rally entry). To become Chartered it needs a junction.

There are 48 junctions on a server. Twelve are lit at launch, held by elder guilds, NPC-run and as mortal as any house. The other 36 are dark. Each Thaw, the Compact holds the Allotment: dark junctions are awarded to applying Companies by Standing, with a Deep Run to relight each one, and a guild that cannot relight its junction by the Writ season forfeits it. A chartered guild that loses its hall's field (a failed Dark season, a dead Master with no succession) goes dark and returns to the Allotment.

A hall has rooms to build, in Notes and materials, and each room is a capability: the hearth (shades, rites, swearing), the yard (Stages, sparring), the archive (founding, Marks), the dock (airship routes), the infirmary (recovery speed), the kitchen (Atmosphere), quarters (roster cap). Elder halls come with all of it and three hundred years of shades.

## The roster

A guild holds up to 60 player characters and 30 NPC members. NPC members come to the door on their own, drawn by Atmosphere, and they are the guild's lives: they take quests with players, hold the hall when players are away, marry, feud, have children who grow up in the kitchen, and die on the line if a quest with their name on it fails. The Master is a player or an NPC; so is every officer.

| Officer | Holds | Decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quartermaster | The treasury, rent, gear | Spending, tenancy |
| Hearthkeeper | The hearth, the shades, the rites | Who swears, who retires, whose shade is kept |
| Yardmaster | Training, Rally roster | Who fights in the Rally, Stage sign-offs |
| Archivist | Marks, founding, the Chronicle entry | Provisional archetypes, what the hall records |

One officer seat per account. An NPC may hold any seat, and a guild of three players and nine NPCs is a real guild.

## Atmosphere

Atmosphere is a tracked value from 0 to 100 that decides who comes to the door, how NPC members behave, and the Rally home advantage. It is made of six things, each visible on the Master's desk: the kitchen, the yard, the hearth's shades, the mix of Shapes under the roof, active feuds, and how the last person who left was treated. Atmosphere drops when a named member dies and nobody names them at the hearth, when a tenant is evicted, when the hall skips a season's rite. It is the game's measure of whether this is a place people would live.

## Standing and tiers

Standing is the guild's score with the Compact. It is earned by Linework, Rally results, crisis contributions, and relit junctions, and it decays 10% a season so that prominence must be kept.

| Tier | Needs | Gets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company | A Writ of Founding | Quests, lodging, Commencement recruiting |
| Chartered | A junction, lit | A hall, a hearth, Rally entry, tenants |
| Great | A junction and top-ten Standing on the server | Writs from every house, a voice in the Allotment, a second junction allowed |
| Seat | Top-three Standing at the third Dark | A seat at the Charter Convention; the guild's name on the term |

## Rallies and guild wars

The Rally is the guild games: three against three in Compact tunnels, every Friday night in the Rally season and in a shorter Friday series the other three seasons. Rally results are Standing and the one legal way to take a junction: a Chartered guild may challenge another's junction in the Rally season, best of three, with the Compact as witness. Writ wars are the other: a house hires one guild against another in a Red zone, flagged, consensual on both sides, with Favor and Standing on the line and named members at risk.

## Succession

When a Master dies, retires, or is removed by the Charter's own process, succession runs by the Charter: an heir, a council vote, or a Thaw election held early. A guild with no succession in 14 days is in interregnum: no Writs, no Rally, Atmosphere falling. The hearth's shades have opinions about succession and say them.

## Dissolution

A guild dissolves when the roster drops below three accounts for a season, when it forfeits its last junction and its last tenancy, or by its own Charter's vote. Its shades go to the Academy hearth, its founded archetypes keep its name, and its Chronicle entry closes with the date.
