# Houses, Charter and Compact

The houses are the politics, the Compact is the law of the Lines, and the Charter is the rules of the game itself, which the server rewrites once a term. Houses are bloodlines with seats, heirs, and wants; they court guilds with Writs, feud with each other through Writ wars, and die when their blood runs out.

## The five houses as factions

Each house is a living faction with a seat, an heir, a standing, a Writ style, and a fate. Its named members are on the roster of the named and can die.

## Favor

Favor is a per-house meter per character from −100 to 100. It is earned by Writs and Escorts for the house, spent on writs of passage, bloodline registration, Ledger weight, and house gear, and lost by working for a rival or failing a Writ. A character's three houses of highest Favor are shown on the character, which the hall reads.

| Favor | The house |
| --- | --- |
| 60 and above | Offers private Writs; sends gifts; names you in its own Chronicle paragraph |
| 20 to 59 | Posts Writs to your board; sells house gear |
| −19 to 19 | Knows your name |
| −20 to −59 | Refuses Writs; its zones are hostile to you |
| −60 and below | Hires against you: your name appears on a rival guild's Writ |

A guild has house relations too, five meters that average its members' Favor with a bonus for Writs the guild completed as a guild. House relations decide which houses recruit from the guild at Commencement and which send it Writ wars.

## Writs

A Writ is a house's quest, posted to boards of guilds the house knows, with the house's terms and a decision timer: Captaincy 2 hours, Council 12, Commons 24. Accepting a Writ for one house is a mark against its rival, and the rival knows. Writs in the Writ season carry crisis weight. A Commons guild may refuse a Writ without losing Favor; any other Charter loses 5 with the house for a refusal.

## Feuds and Writ wars

Houses feud as the Chronicle records it, and in the Writ season a feud can go to Writ war: a house posts a Writ naming a rival's hired guild and a Red zone, the rival's guild is notified, and if both accept, the zone is flagged for the week. Inside, the two parties are hostile, named NPC members on both sides are at risk, Sworn characters can die, and the outcome moves Favor, Standing, and the Ledger. A Writ war a guild refuses is a Writ it failed.

## Succession and the death of houses

A house's heir is a named NPC and can die: in a Writ war, in a crisis ending, in a duel at the Charter seat. A house with no heir has a season to name one from its bloodline, from the server's bloodline NPCs or by adopting a bloodline character's NPC child. A house with no heir at the season turn ends, with the consequences in the table above, and the Chronicle writes its last page. New houses can rise: a Seat guild whose Master is a bloodline NPC may petition the Convention to be made a house, which is the only way a player-founded guild becomes blood.

## The Compact

The Linewright Compact charters the junctions, posts Linework, keeps the registry, and publishes the Ledgers. It is not a house and has no blood; it is a clerkship, and its clerk narrates the Chronicle. The Compact's rules at launch:

| Rule | Launch setting |
| --- | --- |
| Allotment | Each Thaw; dark junctions to applying Companies by Standing; a Deep Run to relight; forfeit by the Writ season |
| Tolls | A Chartered guild pays 5% of Notes earned on its lines; a Commons pays 10% |
| Registry | Bloodlines need a writ above Green; writs cost 20 Favor from any house |
| Rally challenge | A junction may be challenged in Rally season, best of three |
| Writ wars | Legal in Writ season only |
| Ledgers | Published Wednesdays from week 4 |
| Canonization | 12 per season |
| Instructor seats | 12 per term |

## The Charter and the Convention

The Charter is the Compact's rules plus the houses' standing, and every third Dark the Convention amends it. Seats: the three Seat guilds, every surviving house, and the Compact clerk, who votes only to break a tie. Each seat proposes one amendment from the bounded list; the Convention passes up to three by majority; the next term runs under them. The bounded list is the set of things the game can change without breaking:

1. The Commons toll, up or down
2. Registry: enforce, abolish, or the guild exemption
3. Canonization cap: 6, 12, or 24 a season
4. Instructor seats: 6, 12, or 24 a term
5. Allotment by Standing, by lottery, or by Rally
6. Writ wars legal in two seasons, one, or none
7. Rally challenges for junctions on or off
8. A new house recognized from a Seat guild
9. The Charter seat assigned to a house, the Compact, or the Seat guilds jointly
10. A dark line scheduled for relighting (one per term)
11. A new airship route opened (one per term)
12. Shades per hearth: 12 or 24

The Convention is also a scene: a week in the Dark when the Seat guilds' Masters sit in Columbia's Charter hall with the heirs of the houses, and the server watches the votes on the Ledger board.
