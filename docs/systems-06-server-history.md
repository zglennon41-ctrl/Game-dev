# Server History

A server is a world with a history, and the history is the players'. Everything a season decides, every archetype founded, every junction lit, every guild that rose and every house that ended is written to the Chronicle and read back by the systems, so that after a Charter term two servers that launched on the same day are two different countries.

## The state a server carries

The server's world state is a short list of things, each of which the systems read.

| State | Written by | Read by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The line map: which of 48 junctions are lit, who holds each, which lines are dark, which zones exist, which are sealed | Allotments, Rallies, crisis endings, failed Dark seasons | The board, travel, the Academy's lesson 5, every zone's variant |
| The houses: which exist, who holds the Charter seat, each house's heir and standing, feuds | Crisis endings, Writ wars, deaths | Writs, Favor, the Convention's seats |
| The canon: the server's archetypes, base plus founded, with founders' names and restrictions | Founding, canonization, crisis endings 8 and 9 | The Archive, the Academy's lesson 4, every build |
| The roster of the named: which named NPCs are alive, where they are, who they belong to | Quest failures, crisis endings, marriages, succession | Every guild's roster, the Omens, the Echoes |
| The guilds: tiers, Standing, Charters, junctions, Seat guilds | Everything guilds do | The Allotment, the Rally, the Convention |
| The Charter: the rules as amended | The Convention | The rules themselves |
| The Chronicle: the pages | All of the above | Players, Echoes, the Academy's history lesson |

## The Chronicle

The Chronicle is the server's readable history: one page per season, written by the system from the Ledgers and the record, in the voice of the Compact's clerk, plus the guild entries (each guild's page, which the Archivist can annotate) and the roll of the named. It is public on the server and readable from the Academy before a character enrolls, so a player choosing a server can read what it has become. Every character's name goes in the roll with what the record holds: cohort, guild, archetypes founded, Marks, retired or Sworn and dead, the crisis zones they fought in.

## How servers diverge

Divergence is cumulative, and the first term is designed so the big forks come early.

| By the end of | A server has decided |
| --- | --- |
| Year 1 | Whether the Cahokia line is open, under a house toll, or airship-only; which house holds the Charter seat; whether the elder guilds still hold their junctions; which halls went dark in the first winter |
| Year 2 | Whether bloodlines are registered, free, or guild-vouched; whether House Key exists; who holds the central junctions; what came up from the Reef and which Discipline joined the Academy |
| Year 3 | Whether the Unfounded was purged, canonized, or spread; who holds the seat after the Succession; which hearths were relit; the Charter amendments the next term runs under |

Under these sit the slow differences: the canon, which by year three holds forty to a hundred founded archetypes with names no other server has; the guilds, which are the server's actual power structure; and the roll of the dead, which is who the hearths remember.

## What a new character sees

A cohort graduating in year three on a three-year-old server enters a world the bible never described. Lesson 4 offers archetypes founded by players, taught by Instructors who are players. The line map has lit junctions held by guilds with Chronicle pages, and dark ones with stories about why. The board's Writs come from the houses that survived. The hall they join has shades of characters who died in crisis 4. The Academy's history lesson, a short one, tells them the server's twelve pages, and the Echoes let them walk the climaxes. They are not catching up; they are arriving.

## Prominence and the record

Prominence is what the Chronicle makes visible: the Seat guilds of each term, the founders of canon archetypes, the Masters who held junctions through a Dark, the Sworn who died in crisis zones. Prominence has no mechanical reward beyond what the systems already give (Standing, Marks, Instructor seats); its reward is the page. The roll is sortable by anyone, and the Academy's quad has a wall.

## Cross-server caravans

Servers are separate worlds, and characters never move between them. Once a season, at the Rally turn, Timbuktu's caravan carries canon between servers: each server's Archivists (the Great guilds' officers) vote one founded archetype to send and one to receive from a list of what other servers offer. A received archetype arrives as a provisional with the origin server named, and must be canonized here. Caravans are how a great idea on one server becomes a tradition on others, and they are the only thing that crosses.

## The term's end

At the third Dark, the Convention writes the amendments, the Chronicle closes the term's volume, and the server's listing is rewritten in the Compact clerk's voice: a paragraph on what this world is now. New terms begin with a new slate of twelve crises, written with the Chronicles of every server in hand.
