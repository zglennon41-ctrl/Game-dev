# WILDLINE — Design Bible

Nov 4, 2026 · @Zak

*A real-time party RPG on the Instar Earth: PSO's hub-and-excursion loop, Game of Thrones' houses and consequences, Fairy Tail's guild, and a magic system grounded enough that players argue about it for years.*

## Overview

Wildline is a real-time party RPG about a guild that keeps a rail junction alive in the wild between the fortress cities of a magical Earth. You take quests from a hall full of loud, singular people, ride the line out with two friends and three geniuses, run a 20–40 minute excursion, and come home to a hearth where the dead still teach. Nothing in the world is safe, including the people you love.

The fantasy in one sentence: you are a member of the best, strangest guild on the line, and what you are is visible on your body, your beast, and your name.

**Five pillars**

1. **The hall is home.** A roster of individuals with lives, feuds, marriages, and deaths; a hearth with the shades of the dead; a guild mark you choose where to wear. (Fairy Tail)
2. **Every rule has a reason.** Will, Shapes, Stages, Terms, Blood: one logic all the way down, legible enough that players argue about it for years. (Hunter × Hunter)
3. **Excursions are the game.** Hub, board, party, line, zone, home. Three players, three geniuses, loot and reputation, then the hall has changed. (Phantasy Star Online)
4. **Consequences are real.** Houses, the Charter, named deaths, succession, and a political map that moves whether you are watching it or not. (Game of Thrones)
5. **What you are is visible.** Blood, Shape, genius, gear and genre show on the character. Jobs are what the world remembers, and players can found new ones. (Guild Wars)

**A day on the line**

You wake in your room over the platform at Cumberland Junction and come down to the common room. The board has nine quests: four Linework jobs from the Compact, two Hunts, a Salvage run into the Shenandoah Ruin, an Escort, and a Writ from House Key with no details and a lot of money. Hollis is already arguing for the Writ. Oona is not speaking to Hollis. Pell is asleep under the table with his wolf, which is also him.

You take the Salvage run with Oona and Sister Yemisi. On the platform you check gear, swap a technique, and board. The ride out is ninety seconds of the three of you and your geniuses in the car, and Yemisi tells you what the ruin used to be: a road, the old kind, for machines with nobody's will in them. The zone is four areas and a thing at the end that was a bridge once. Oona holds the line while you Project; Yemisi reads the field and calls the break; your genius takes the hit you did not see. Perfect chain on your Pattern, Terms paid in full, and the bridge-thing comes down.

Home. Loot sorted, witness marks logged, your genius a little stranger than it was. In the common room Hollis took the Writ alone and came back with a house ring and a look. At the hearth, one of the shades will not speak to Hollis any more. The Compact has posted a notice: the Cahokia line went quiet at mile 140. Tam Pilot is from the Cahokia line. Tam has already left.

## The World

Earth, ten thousand years after the Awakening, when the will of living things became a physical force. Twelve fortress cities stand on the old rivers, the wild lies between them, and the Lines hold the whole thing together.

**The Awakening**

Our world ended quietly. One spring, every living thing began to project a field: the shape of its will, pressing on the world. Crops grew toward whoever wanted them most. Predators learned to make prey freeze. Cities became storms of ten million wills, and the ones that survived did it by merging into fortresses whose walls are resonance fields, the summed will of everyone inside, held in a shape. The old world survives as ruins, as the Quiet (places where no field has ever returned), and as artifacts nobody fully understands. Every fortress tells the same founding story twelve different ways. The Academy at Alexandria still trains the cities' awakened; the Wildline is where the ones who leave it end up.

**The Wild**

Between the fortresses, fields are unstable. Weather has intent. Beasts evolved at their life-cycle thresholds under raw will and kept evolving, so a wolf out here has molted four times and the fourth form is nobody's wolf. The wild is where the old world is, the ruins and the Quiet, and where the strong-blooded go when the cities register them. Nobody lives in the wild except linesmen, outlaws, exiles, and things.

**The Lines**

Rail, mostly underground, cut by the first fortresses when the surface was unlivable. Junction halls hold the tunnels open: a guild's resonance field is what keeps the wild out of the tracks, and when a hall's field fails, the line goes dark. The Linewright Compact charters the junctions across every city it reaches and pays the guilds that keep them. Airships cross what rail cannot, oceans and the deep wild, and the airship houses are a power of their own. Lines are graded by color: Green, Amber, Red, Black.

**The houses and the Charter**

Columbia's Charter governs the Lines in the Americas, and Columbia's houses are bloodlines in the literal sense: a house's standing is the strength of what it breeds. The cities register strong blood, and a registered bloodline needs a Compact writ to travel outside the walls, which is the first thing most of the Wildline's members broke.

| House | Blood | Seat | Wants | Toward the Junction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Calvert | Winged | The Aerie, above the Potomac falls | The airship lanes; the Compact's charter | Buys the guild's silence; owes it a debt it resents |
| Carroll | Dragon-blooded | Carrollton, the old manor-fort | The Charter seat; a war to settle the houses | Wants the Junction as a vassal; will take it as a ruin |
| Howard | Shapechangers | Nowhere anyone can prove | Secrets; every house's secrets | Has a member inside the guild; the guild does not know which |
| Key | Demon-blooded | The Night Fort on the bay | Its runaway heir back, alive or not | Hires the guild for Writs no one else will take |
| Banneker | Demigod-blooded | The Observatory Hill | The truth about the Lines, and the Quiet | The guild's only honest friend, and the most dangerous |

The Compact sits above the houses on paper and below them in practice. No house is safe. Heirs die in duels, lords die in the wild, and a house that loses its last strong-blooded child is a house that ends.

**Cumberland Junction**

The Junction sits in the Appalachian spine where three things meet: the Columbia line from the east, the Cahokia line from the west, and the Mountain Dock, where airships from Lunden make landfall. The Wildline guild has held it for three hundred years, in a hall built into the old station: a common room with the board, a hearth that holds the shades of every member who died on the line, a yard, an archive, a platform, a dock, and rooms upstairs. The guild mark is a rail spike through a ring, and members wear it where their blood shows.

## How It Plays

The loop is hub, board, party, line, excursion, home: 20–40 minutes per run, and the hall and the political map change between runs.

1. **Common room.** Read the board, the rumors, and the roster. Who is in, who is hurt, who is not speaking to whom.
2. **Take a quest.** Each posting shows the line color, the poster, the pay, and which named members are at risk if it goes wrong.
3. **Form the party.** Three: you plus two friends online, or two roster members whose own lives decide whether they will come.
4. **Platform.** Gear, consumables, technique loadout, genius orders. Last chance to change your Pattern.
5. **The car.** Ninety seconds out: the party and three geniuses in one rail car, briefing, dialogue, the thing nobody wants to say.
6. **The zone.** Three to five areas, a resonance post at the midpoint, a boss or an objective at the end.
7. **Extraction.** The ride home, with whatever you carried out and whoever you carried.
8. **The hearth.** Loot, witness marks, genius growth, the roster's reactions, the map's response. Then the board again, changed.

**The Junction hub**

| Room | What you do there |
| --- | --- |
| Common room | The board, the roster, rumors, the Master's notices, the fights that do not happen in the yard |
| Hearth | Rites, swearing Terms before witnesses, speaking with shades, naming the dead |
| Yard | Training the Stages, sparring, the rite that finds your Shape, Rally practice |
| Archive | The archetype canon, founding, witness marks, old-world artifacts and what they mean |
| Platform | Lines out, line status, the car |
| Mountain Dock | Airship routes, opens in Act 2 |
| Outfitter, Smith, Apothecary | Gear by city genre, weapon work, consumables and field-salts |
| Infirmary | Recovery, the surgeon who is also the guild's bookkeeper |
| Quarters | Your room: appearance, trophies, the genius's bed, letters |
| Master's office | Guild standing, house relations, Compact contracts, succession |

**Quest types**

| Type | Posted by | What it is | What it pays in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Linework | The Compact | Hold or clear a tunnel section, restore a resonance post | Guild standing, line safety, steady money |
| Salvage | The Archive, the cities | Old-world ruins; bring back what the Archive wants | Gear, lore, archetype fragments |
| Hunt | Towns, houses | A beast with a name and a bounty | Materials, genius growth |
| Escort | The Compact, houses | A train or airship through danger | Money, house favor |
| Writ | A house | Political work: retrieve, arrest, protect, embarrass, disappear | House reputation up with one house, down with another |
| Deep Run | The guild | Multi-stage runs into the deep wild where no line goes | The best gear, the story, the Quiet |
| Rally | The guild games | Guild against guild in the tunnels, seasonal | Prestige, recruits, bragging rights at every junction |

**Excursions**

Zones are built from handcrafted tiles with fixed set pieces, PSO fashion, so a line you have run ten times is familiar and never identical. Each zone has a genre from the territory it crosses: Columbia's ruins, Cahokia's drowned country, Calvert aeries, the Quiet. The resonance post at the midpoint is the checkpoint, the regroup, and the place where the party decides whether to push to the boss or extract with what it has. Bosses are beasts past their fourth molt, old-world machines with a will in them, or people.

**Combat**

Third-person, real time, lock-on, with a health bar and a Will bar. Every character has four verbs, and they are the four Stages of the field made into buttons.

| Verb | What it does | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hold | Guard; a perfect-timed Hold is a parry that refunds Will | Will while held |
| Shape | Enter your Shape's stance: Anchor hits harder, Cast moves faster, Turn changes element, Make summons, Bind controls, Read slows the world | Will per second |
| Project | Attacks and techniques: weapon strikes, ranged shots, the technique list of your Shape and Discipline | Will per technique |
| Pattern | Your signature: a chain of three to seven inputs on the pulse, Terms paid in full | Will, plus the Term's Toll |

The pulse is a ring around your feet that beats with your field. Techniques land on beats. A Pattern is a Legend of Dragoon chain: hit every beat and the Terms multiplier pays out whole; miss one and the Pattern fizzles and the Toll is still taken. Weapons carry their city's genre and fighting rhythm: a Columbia musket is slow, loud, and wants a Cast; an Edo blade wants the beat exactly; a Colonia engine-arm is a stance of its own.

Beasts have Shapes too, and the Shape wheel is the counter system: a Bind-shaped beast is broken by Turn, a Make-shaped construct by Cast, and so on around the wheel. Reading a beast's Shape before it closes is the first skill the yard teaches.

**Party and genius**

Three players, three geniuses, six bodies in the zone. Roster members fight the way they are: Oona holds, Hollis overcommits, Pell is a wolf. Your genius takes five orders.

| Order | The genius |
| --- | --- |
| Guard | Stays at your side, blocks what you do not see |
| Hunt | Focuses your lock-on target |
| Hold | Takes and keeps a position or a post |
| Free | Uses its own judgment, which is yours from a year ago |
| Duet | Joins your Pattern, Grown geniuses only; the chain doubles in length and payout |

**Line colors**

| Color | Who it is for | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Green | Stage 2 members | Beasts fight simply; posts are frequent |
| Amber | Stage 3 with a Pattern | Beasts read your Shape and counter it; fewer posts |
| Red | Sworn Terms recommended | Beasts past the fourth molt; named members can die here |
| Black | Sworn Terms required | Everything is a person, a machine, or something from the Quiet; opens after the campaign |

**Death and consequence**

A downed player can be revived by the party within sixty seconds, or is carried home and keeps only witness marks. A fallen genius recovers at the hearth, but one that falls three times in a season loses a Stage. Named roster members on a quest you fail can die, permanently, and the board tells you who is at risk before you accept. The roster is finite. Members marry, feud, leave for a house, come back, and the Master can die, which starts a succession the whole hall has opinions about.

**Multiplayer**

The Junction is the lobby, PSO style, with up to 24 players visible in the hall and parties of three on the line. Drop-in, cross-play, and solo play with roster members as AI. Rallies are three against three in Compact tunnels. Founded archetypes propagate across the server through the Archive.

**Guild management**

Light, real, and in service of the loop. The roster holds about thirty members at launch and grows by recruitment, apprentices, and children. The hall has rooms to build. The Atmosphere of the hall is a tracked value made of the kitchen, the yard, the feuds, and how you treated the last person who left, and it decides who comes to your door. House relations are five meters that move with every Writ. Compact standing decides which lines you may run. The Rally season runs four times a year.

## The Logic

One rule sits under everything: a living thing's will is a physical field, and everything a character can do is that field held, shaped, projected, or patterned, within the limits of the blood they were born with. Three layers, each with its own rule, together make the character.

**Will**

Everyone has a field. Untrained, it leaks, which is why beasts find children and why the yard's first lesson is Hold. At puberty the field settles into its Shape, and the genius settles with it: that is the Awakening, and it is the same event for a Columbia marshal's daughter and a wolf in the Allegheny dark.

**The six Shapes**

A Shape is the direction your field bends. There are six, in three opposing pairs, found at the rite: you hold the hearth bowl, still water on the hearthstone, and the water answers.

| Shape | The field | Combat identity | The bowl shows | Opposite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Anchor | Stays in the body | Strength, endurance, melee, the wall | The water rises and overflows | Read |
| Turn | Changes its nature | Elements, properties, conversion, poison and fire | The water changes color or taste | Bind |
| Cast | Leaves the body | Ranged, projection, speed, the shot | The water leaps out of the bowl | Make |
| Read | Perceives | Senses, foresight, detection, finding the break, setting the field right (healing) | You see something in the water | Anchor |
| Bind | Attaches to others | Control, puppetry, contracts, debuffs, the leash | The water moves toward your hand | Turn |
| Make | Takes independent form | Constructs, conjured weapons, tools, the summon | A shape forms in the water | Cast |

The wheel runs Anchor, Turn, Cast, Read, Bind, Make, and back to Anchor. You use your own Shape at full strength, the two beside it at 80%, the two beyond at 60%, and your opposite at 40%. A Turn can learn fire well, a shot passably, and a leash badly. This is also the beast counter system: break a beast by hitting it from the Shape its own Shape is weak to.

Read the ring as the counter system too: a beast is broken from the Shape its own Shape is weakest to, straight across the wheel.

**The four Stages**

Stages are learned in order, in the yard, and each one is a combat verb.

| Stage | Name | What it is | Where it is taught |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hold | Contain the field: stop leaking, guard, parry | First week at the Junction |
| 2 | Shape | Give the field your Shape's quality; output doubles | Green lines |
| 3 | Project | Extend the field past the skin: techniques, the real fighting | Amber lines |
| 4 | Pattern | A technique that is yours, built on a Term | The hearth, before witnesses |

Two advanced techniques come after: **Still**, closing the field entirely (invisible to beasts, no Will regeneration, no genius), and **Fold**, pressing the whole field into one part of the body for a burst or a pinpoint guard.

**Terms**

A Pattern is your Shape, a Form chosen from your Shape's technique list, and up to three Terms. A Term is a condition you accept, and the condition is the power.

| Term | What it is | Examples | Multiplier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trigger | When the Pattern may be used | Only after I have been struck; only below half health; only on the seventh beat; only at night | ×1.5 |
| Toll | What it costs beyond Will | No weapon for the rest of the run; the genius sits out; one witness mark burned; a day of silence | ×2 |
| Bar | What it may never do | Never against the unarmed; never at a woman; never twice on one foe; never in the hall | ×1.5 |

The grammar is 20 Triggers, 20 Tolls, and 15 Bars, so there are thousands of Patterns and each one reads as a sentence. Swear the Terms at the hearth before three witnesses and the Pattern's multiplier doubles; break a sworn Term and the Pattern is gone for good and the shades know. Break an unsworn Term and the Pattern is gone for a season. Everyone's Terms are private, and learning someone's Terms is intimacy; using them against that person is the worst thing a member can do.

Three Patterns from the roster:

- **Last Word** (Dashiell Key, Cast). A shot that does not miss. Trigger: only after he has been shot at. Toll: he cannot draw a gun again that day. Bar: never at someone unarmed.
- **Ridgeback** (Oona Flint, Anchor). She cannot be moved from where she stands. Trigger: only with a member behind her. Toll: she cannot attack while it holds. Sworn.
- **Eleven Bells** (Hollis Carroll, Turn). Her blade becomes whatever element last struck her. Trigger: only on the eleventh beat of a fight. Toll: the genius sits the fight out. Unsworn, because Hollis does not swear.

**Blood**

Blood is what you were born carrying. It sets your raw material and your Gap, the thing the line cannot do.

| Tier | What it is | Examples | What it gives | The Gap | How common |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mark | A single mutation, yours alone | Eyes that see heat; a second heart; a voice beasts obey | A free bonus that foretells your Pattern | Small, often a weakness to one Shape | Most people |
| Kindred | A congenital set shared by a people | The Ridgefolk, stone-skinned; the Tidewater, who breathe the bay; the Fogborn of Lunden | A Gift the whole people share | A Gap the whole people share | Ten or more peoples per city; twelve in the mountains at launch |
| Bloodline | Strong blood, registered by the cities | Winged, dragon-blooded, shapechanger, demon-blooded, demigod, fused | A Trunk of techniques no one else can learn | Large, and a thing the line can never do | Rare; the houses |

A Pattern can reach into the Gap only by a sworn Term, which is where the great characters live: the dragon-blooded who swore never to fly so she could breathe cold.

**Genius**

Your genius is the field made into a companion, and it is read, not chosen. At creation you pick a Seed, a one-line leaning (something that watches from above; something I can hold; something that was never alive), and at the rite the genius takes its First Form from the Seed, your blood, and your Shape. It can be a beast, a small humanoid, a sentient engine, a relic you wear or wield, a demon, or the field with no body at all. It has six forms over a life, and every evolution is a Reading: the game reads how you have actually played since the last one and offers three new base forms that carry a few features of the old, so a hawk seeded by a player who turns out to fight close becomes a hawk-headed warrior or a talon gauntlet with the hawk's eyes. From the Third Form it develops secondary forms (a mount, a weapon, a ward, a war-form) when conditions are met. The genius is the field and the Pattern is the field's intention, and Resonance binds them: an evolution strengthens the Pattern, a Pattern tier evolves the genius, and neither side loses the power of the one you neglected. The Systems doc's Genius tab has the whole of it.

When a member dies, the genius goes to the hearth as a shade. Shades keep their partner's Patterns and teach them to members they accept, and a shade's acceptance is a social system: it has opinions about who you are. Shades fade if the hall's field fails, which is the real reason a dark line is a tragedy. The fused, born with no separate genius, have the beast's power in their own body, cannot Still, and cannot hear the shades; they are the guild's most feared and loneliest members.

**Archetypes: the job system**

An archetype is a job the world remembers. Its definition is a Shape crossed with a Discipline, one of the twelve city schools, so a Cast Marshal and a Cast Blade are kin and nothing alike. Six Shapes by twelve Disciplines is seventy-two base archetypes; bloodline Trunks add variants on each (a dragon-blooded Warden breathes), and founded archetypes add the rest.

| Rank | How you earn it | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Journeyman | Take the archetype at the Archive | Its stance and first three techniques |
| Adept | Ten witness marks in it on the line | Its full technique list, its gear affinity |
| Master | Fifty marks and a Pattern of your own in it | Its mastery technique; you can teach it |

Your build is eight slots, Guild Wars fashion: one Pattern, four techniques, two stances, and one Duet. Mastered techniques come with you when you switch, so the tree is a history of where you have been.

**Founding**

This is the system that makes it the greatest. A Pattern with the same Shape, Form, and Terms used by three members on witnessed quests becomes a named archetype. The Archive records it, Timbuktu's library canonizes it, the founder's name is on it forever, and any player on the server can take it as a job, Terms included. The job list starts at seventy-two and grows by what players make. A hundred years from now, the canon is the history of everyone who ever played.

**Customization**

1. Blood: tier, then kindred or bloodline, then your Mark.
2. City of origin: your gear genre, your first Discipline, your accent.
3. Genius: base form, which the Shape will later color.
4. The rite: the bowl finds your Shape. You do not pick it; you can retake the rite once, in Act 3, at a cost.
5. Your first Pattern's Terms, which you will outgrow.
6. Appearance, with the blood always visible; the guild mark's placement; your room.

## Story and Content

Five acts across the guild's hardest year: a line goes dark, a house blames the Junction, the houses go to war, and an archetype nobody founded appears at every hearth on earth. The player is a new member, and the ending depends on who is still alive.

Thirty members; these ten carry the story. Any of them can die.

**The Unfounded**

The endgame's antagonist is a job. An archetype with a Shape no one has, a Discipline from no city, and Terms no one swore, recorded in every Archive at once. Geniuses learn it by Witness without anyone using it. The player's last Pattern is sworn against it, with the hall as witnesses, and the ending asks whether a thing the world remembers can be forgotten.

## Building It

Build the pulse and the Terms first, on paper if need be, because everything else in the game is downstream of whether a Pattern feels like a vow.

**Production pillars**

1. **Readable at a glance.** Blood, Shape, and genius are visible in silhouette. A player should know what someone is before they speak.
2. **PS2 clarity, painted.** Clean forms, strong color, hand-painted surfaces over simple geometry. Appalachian Gothic for the Junction: iron, timber, lamplight, fog in the cut. Each city's genre is a palette and a gear language, not a new engine.
3. **The pulse is the music.** Every zone's score is written on the player's pulse tempo. Appalachian strings in the hall; the cities bring their own.
4. **Handcrafted tiles, fixed set pieces.** PSO's approach: zones that are familiar and never identical, built from a tile library per territory.
5. **One roster, written like people.** Thirty members with a page each: wants, fears, a Term, a secret, a death they would accept.

**Prototype order**

| Step | Build | Proves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Paper: the Term grammar and a Pattern builder as cards, played with a fate deck | Whether Terms produce Patterns people want to swear |
| 1 | A combat room: one Shape (Anchor), Hold and Project, the pulse ring, one beast | Whether beat-timed melee feels good |
| 2 | The Pattern: a 5-beat chain with one Term and its multiplier | Whether the vow pays off in the hand |
| 3 | One Green zone: three areas, a post, a boss | Whether the loop is 20–40 minutes and worth repeating |
| 4 | The genius as an AI follower with Guard, Hunt, and Witness | Whether players care about the beast |
| 5 | The hearth: the rite, one shade, one sworn Term | Whether the hall is a place |
| 6 | Common room, board, six roster members with lives, one named death | Whether consequence lands |
| 7 | Co-op for three on the Green zone | Whether it is a party game |
| 8 | Blood tiers, the second Shape, the Archive and founding | Whether the job system is the greatest |

Steps 0 to 3 are a vertical slice. If the slice is not fun with one Shape and one zone, nothing added later will fix it.
